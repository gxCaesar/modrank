\begin{abstract}
\noindent Multimodal models combining whole-slide images with tumour transcriptomes are ranked for bladder
cancer survival on one public benchmark against a clinical reference built from tumour grade. In
muscle-invasive disease nearly every tumour is high grade. Rebuilding the reference with pathologic
stage from the benchmark's own files raised its concordance from 0.567 to 0.664 and more than halved
the apparent added value of three multimodal models, a bias that replicated in an independent
cohort. We introduce ModRank, which fits one penalised Cox model per modality and averages their
ranks without fitting the combination. It reached 0.721, the highest value reported on the
benchmark, exceeded concatenated and learned fusion on identical inputs in all 24 re-partitions and
kept its concordance when whole hospitals were held out. In three external cohorts without
histology, the two-modality combination did not exceed stage beyond the noise floor. Clinical
references for this disease should include pathologic stage.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}

Bladder cancer causes more than 200,000 deaths a year worldwide~\citep{bray2024globocan}. For the
quarter of patients who present with muscle-invasive disease and are treated by radical cystectomy,
the pathologic stage of the specimen is the strongest routine predictor of what happens after
surgery, and it guides whether adjuvant treatment is considered and how closely the patient is
followed~\citep{witjes2021eau}. Stage is also a coarse instrument. Two patients with the same pT3N0
disease receive the same recommendation and do not share an outcome, and the pathology report holds
nothing that separates them. The residual heterogeneity is real. Transcriptomic classes of
muscle-invasive tumours differ markedly in survival and cut across stage~\citep{kamoun2020consensus,
robertson2017blca}, and the morphology of the resected tumour carries information no staging
category encodes. What a model should add, then, is what stage leaves unsaid.

Multimodal deep learning answers that with the slide and the transcriptome together. Weakly
supervised aggregation over whole-slide tiles made slide-level prediction practical without region
annotation~\citep{lu2021clam, chen2024uni, ding2025titan}, and a family of models now fuses slide
tokens with pathway-level expression through attention, optimal transport or prototype
learning~\citep{chen2021mcat, xu2023motcat, jaume2024survpath, song2024mmp, zhang2024pibd,
eijpe2025dimaf}. Their progress on bladder cancer is measured almost entirely on one benchmark: the
five-fold splits of disease-specific survival that SurvPath released for 359 patients of The Cancer
Genome Atlas~\citep{jaume2024survpath}. Two properties of that benchmark decide what its leaderboard
can mean, and neither has been examined.

The first is the clinical reference. A multimodal model's value is its improvement over what the
clinic already knows, so the clinical arm fixes the zero point of every comparison. On this
benchmark, every method paper we could read that reports a clinical baseline builds it from tumour
grade, and none uses pathologic stage. Muscle-invasive urothelial carcinoma is almost uniformly high
grade: in this cohort 94.4\% of cases share one grade level. A covariate that does not distinguish
the patients cannot separate them, and a grade-based reference therefore understates what the
clinic knows. The multi-omics survival literature settled the equivalent question years ago, with
stage in the reference~\citep{herrmann2021benchmark, wissel2023noise}. The whole-slide literature
inherited its reference from an earlier line of work that used grade~\citep{chen2022porpoise}.

The second is the sample size. Three hundred and fifty-nine patients with 113 deaths from the
disease is a small survival problem, and the events-per-parameter arithmetic behind modern
sample-size guidance argues against fitting tens of millions of parameters to it~\citep{riley2020samplesize}.
A method built for this regime should spend its events where the signal is and none on the
combination.

Here we measure both properties and build a method around them. We show that a stage-based
reference, rebuilt from the benchmark's own files, lifts the clinical arm from 0.5666 to 0.6638
concordance and more than halves the added value that published multimodal models appear to have. We then
introduce ModRank, which fits one penalised Cox model per modality over a frozen foundation-model
slide embedding, pathway-level transcriptomics and stage, and combines them by an equal-weight rank
average with no fitted parameter. Under a protocol frozen before the run it reaches the highest
concordance reported on the benchmark. It exceeds concatenated and learned fusion on identical
inputs in every one of 24 re-partitions, holds its concordance when whole hospitals are held out,
and gives calibrated two-year risks, with a gain in accuracy over the clinical model that this cohort
cannot resolve. We report where it does not
transfer, and why.

\section*{Results}

\subsection*{The benchmark's clinical reference omits pathologic stage}

We read every method paper reported on the bladder cohort of the benchmark. Eight of the eleven
could be read in full. Four report a clinical-variable baseline, all four build it from age, sex and
tumour grade, and none uses pathologic stage. The other four report no clinical baseline at all
(Supplementary Note~1 and Supplementary Table~1). The three unread papers are marked unverified rather than assumed to
follow the pattern. The reported bladder values are 0.519 and 0.570 concordance. Stage is not missing from
the data. It is a column of the files released with the benchmark, and it sits directly beside grade
in the split files of the current best method~\citep{eijpe2025dimaf} (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}a).

The mechanism is specific to this disease. In this cohort grade takes two levels with 94.4\% of
cases in one of them (normalised entropy 0.311), against five levels and 0.665 for stage
(Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}d). We rebuilt the clinical arm twice from the incumbent's own files,
differing in one column. The grade construction reaches 0.5666 and the stage construction 0.6638, a
gap of +0.0972 (95\% confidence interval $[+0.047, +0.149]$, $p<0.001$, 6,000 case-resampling
replicates) on identical cases through an identical pipeline. The stage-based reference exceeds two of the four published entries on verified folds and five of all nine
(Fig.~\ref{fig:missing}a, Table~\ref{tab:published}). The same one-column swap raises the clinical
arm in all five studies the benchmark covers (Fig.~\ref{fig:missing}c). In two of them the released
files carry no usable grade, so there the comparison is with age and sex alone (Supplementary
Table~2).

\subsection*{What limits a method at 359 patients}

Before designing a model we measured three properties of the regime. A ridge Cox model fitted to
pure noise and scored in-sample reaches 0.7474 concordance at sixteen parameters, so an in-sample
fit on this cohort says little about signal. A two-parameter fusion fitted \emph{on the held-out
fold}, an oracle no deployed model could have, is worth only $+0.008$ over equal weighting (0.7268
against 0.7191), and a 21-point weight sweep peaks at exactly equal weight. A fusion gated on
clinical risk loses to its own permuted-stratum control (0.7493 against 0.7502), and none of the seven
components designed on this evidence passed its pre-registered test (Supplementary Note~2,
Supplementary Table~3 and Supplementary Fig.~1). Re-splitting the
same patients 24 times moves a paired difference between arms by a standard deviation of 0.0073, so a
margin must exceed 0.0145 to survive a reader re-running the study (Fig.~\ref{fig:regime}).

The three measurements point the same way. The binding constraint is estimation, not the fusion.
ModRank therefore fits one ridge-penalised Cox model per modality on the training fold (a 768-dimensional
TITAN slide embedding~\citep{ding2025titan}, the benchmark's own 275 pathway groups, and age, sex
and pathologic stage), maps each out-of-fold score to its within-fold percentile, and takes the
equal-weight mean. It carries 1,049 coefficients and fits nothing in the combination
(Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}b).

\subsection*{ModRank leads the benchmark and holds under re-partitioning and held-out hospitals}

Under a protocol frozen before the run, with five leakage falsifiers executed first, ModRank reaches
0.7212 concordance (mean over five seeds, standard deviation 0.0048, Supplementary Table~4), the
highest value reported on
these folds and $+0.0422$ above the best entry whose folds we verified (Table~\ref{tab:published}).
The two competitors whose implementations run end to end were given the same clinical variables by
the same rank average. ModRank exceeds SurvPath by $+0.0340$ ($[-0.0056, +0.0731]$, $p=0.084$,
competitor averaged over five retrainings) and PIBD by $+0.0249$ ($[-0.0181, +0.0675]$, $p=0.241$),
with 1,049 parameters against 24.7 and 26.9 million. Neither margin is significant at this cohort
size, and we read the ordering against published values as descriptive (Fig.~\ref{fig:result}).

The design choice that matters most is fitting one model per modality and not fitting the
combination, and it can be tested on identical inputs. On the same features, folds, seeds and
estimator (Fig.~\ref{fig:robust}a), a single ridge Cox on all three blocks concatenated reaches $0.6864 \pm 0.0063$, and
fusion weights learned on inner out-of-fold scores (stacking) reach $0.7106 \pm 0.0044$. ModRank
exceeds the concatenation by $+0.0340$ ($[+0.0014, +0.0674]$, $p=0.042$) and the stacking by
$+0.0108$ ($[-0.0052, +0.0269]$). Over the 24 random re-partitions on which the noise floor was
measured, ModRank exceeds the concatenation ($+0.0464 \pm 0.0158$), the stacking ($+0.0111 \pm 0.0065$)
and the clinical arm ($+0.0549 \pm 0.0083$) in 24 of 24 (Fig.~\ref{fig:robust}b). When whole tissue
source sites are held out, so that no hospital contributes to both sides of any fold (33 sites, five
folds), ModRank holds 0.7210 while the stacking falls to 0.6855 and the concatenation to 0.6487
(Fig.~\ref{fig:robust}c).

Two further checks bound the result. ModRank reads a 2025 slide encoder that the competitors did not.
Given instead the tile features SurvPath itself consumed, ModRank reaches 0.7009, still above every
published entry, which places the encoder's contribution near 0.022. And the protocol's
pre-registered selection correction was mis-specified. It used the standard deviation of a paired
difference where the maximum-of-candidates correction needs that of a single candidate's score.
Against the correctly measured bar of 0.7716 the primary does not clear it (Supplementary Note~2).

\subsection*{A grade-based reference inflates the added value of every multimodal model}

A clinical reference fixes the zero point of every claim about what histology and transcriptomics
add, so we measured how far the reference moves it. For three multimodal constructions (ModRank, and
SurvPath and PIBD each given the clinical block), we computed the added value over each reference,
$C(\text{model} + \text{reference}) - C(\text{reference})$, and the inflation $D$, the difference
between the added value over grade and over stage, with all four concordances recomputed on each of
6,000 case resamples.

Against the grade-based reference every construction appears to add a great deal: $+0.1204$
($[+0.0684, +0.1708]$) for ModRank, $+0.0670$ for SurvPath and $+0.0841$ for PIBD. Against the stage-based
reference the added values fall to $+0.0594$, $+0.0253$ and $+0.0345$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:added}a). The inflation is significant for all three, $D = +0.0610$ ($[+0.0284,
+0.0955]$), $+0.0417$ ($[+0.0196, +0.0668]$) and $+0.0496$ ($[+0.0238, +0.0769]$), and for ModRank
it is positive in 24 of 24 re-partitions ($+0.0628 \pm 0.0086$). Against the stage-based reference, only ModRank's
added value has an interval that excludes zero (Fig.~\ref{fig:added}a,~b). That comparison does not
survive correction across the six prespecified comparisons (Holm $q=0.065$), and we report it as the
direction the evidence points rather than a resolved difference.

\subsection*{Calibration and clinical utility}

ModRank emits a rank, so absolute risk has to be learned on top of it without touching a validation
outcome. Inside each training fold we fitted one Cox coefficient on the training cases'
inner-out-of-fold ModRank score and a Breslow baseline hazard, and applied both to the validation
cases. The clinical model received the identical treatment. Pooled over the validation folds, the
mean predicted two-year risk of death from the disease was 0.342 against an observed 0.341, the
calibration slope was 0.770 (clinical model 0.849), and predicted and observed risks agreed across
quintiles (Fig.~\ref{fig:added}c, Supplementary Note~5 and Supplementary Table~8). Against a
training-fold Kaplan--Meier null, the index of
prediction accuracy was 0.098 at one year and 0.152 at two years for ModRank, against 0.047 and 0.079
for the clinical model, and the two were equal at three years (0.105). Resampling patients with the
predictions held fixed, the two-year difference of $+0.0724$ has a 95\% interval of
$[-0.0054, +0.1502]$. At two years ModRank's net benefit exceeded both the clinical model and treating
everyone at every threshold examined from 20 to 60\%, for example 0.136 against 0.090 and $-0.098$ at
40\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:added}d). At 40\% the difference from the clinical model is $+0.0454$
($[+0.0006, +0.0915]$), and at the other four thresholds tested its interval includes zero, so the
utility gain is suggestive rather than established.

\subsection*{What the slide contributes}

The three arms are close to independent. The Spearman correlation between the slide and clinical
scores is 0.2445, and between the incumbent's joint score and the clinical score 0.1924. The slide
earns its place where the report cannot separate patients. On the 5\% of comparable pairs that the
clinical model places closest together (1,213 of 24,219), the clinical arm is at chance (0.4959) and
the slide arm reaches 0.6220 (Fig.~\ref{fig:modalities}d and Supplementary Table~5). On the 3,893
pairs that neither the
transcriptome arm nor the clinical model separates, each placing the two patients within its closest
40\%, the slide arm reaches 0.6212 while those two arms sit at 0.5543 and 0.5634. The two molecular-facing arms read different
biology. The transcriptome arm tracks the basal, luminal and epithelial--mesenchymal axes (Spearman
0.3595, $-0.372$ and 0.3672). The slide arm's correlation with any of the eight standard axes is at
most 0.2245 in magnitude, and its two strongest pathway correlates are metabolic
(Supplementary Note~3 and Supplementary Fig.~2). Across the median split of the
basal-minus-luminal axis, the slide and clinical arms exchange places while ModRank moves far less
(0.7109 and 0.6997, Supplementary Table~6 and Supplementary Fig.~3). Supplementary Figs.~4 and~5
draw the three modalities against one patient ordering and the pre-specified case series with its
histology.

\subsection*{External cohorts}

The benchmark has no held-out bladder cohort, so we tested both findings in independent public
cohorts under protocols frozen before their outcomes were analysed. The public cohorts that combine
survival with bulk expression carry no slides, so these tests concern the transcriptome and clinical
arms. The recipe was refitted inside each cohort by repeated cross-validation.

The inflation from a grade-based reference replicated. In a Swedish cohort of 224 patients with 25
deaths from the disease~\citep{sjodahl2012molecular}, where grade and stage both vary, a grade-based reference inflated the
transcriptome's added value by $D = +0.0581$ ($[+0.0175, +0.0971]$, $p=0.007$). In a cystectomy
cohort of 93 patients with 38 deaths~\citep{riester2012combination}, it was positive without reaching significance ($+0.0253$).
The protocol's sensitivity analysis in that cohort, which excludes the three patients given
chemotherapy before cystectomy, gave $+0.1094$ ($[+0.0424, +0.1774]$). An estimate that three patients
move this far is fragile, and the all-patient value is the primary one.

The combination rule did not replicate beyond each cohort's noise floor. The two-arm ModRank had the
best point estimate in the Swedish cohort (0.8779 against 0.8672 for the stage block and 0.8747 for
stacking) and in a third cohort of 73 patients~\citep{choi2014identification} (0.6384 against 0.6322).
In the Swedish cohort it also exceeded the transcriptome alone and the concatenation after Holm
correction ($p=0.004$ for both). But it did not exceed the clinical stage block by more than the
pre-registered bar in any cohort. In the cystectomy cohort, microarray pathway
scores were uninformative (0.4742), and the equal-weight average fell below the stage block (0.5898
against 0.6590). A variant that admits an arm only when it clears a permutation null inside the
training fold was defined before any of these cohorts was downloaded. It was added by a dated
amendment after the cystectomy cohort's results had been read, so it was tested only on the two
cohorts not yet seen. It equalled ModRank where both arms were informative, moved toward the stage
block where the transcriptome arm was weak, and exceeded the stage block by less than the bar in
both (Supplementary Note~4 and Supplementary Table~7).

\section*{Discussion}

This study makes two findings about how multimodal survival models for bladder cancer are measured
and one about how they should be built. The benchmark's clinical reference omits the routine
pathology variable that separates patients in muscle-invasive disease, and correcting it changes what
every multimodal model appears to add. At 359 patients and 113 events, a method that fits nothing in
its combination is the strongest construction on identical inputs, and it keeps that position when
the same patients are re-partitioned and when whole hospitals are held out.

The inflation is a property of the reference, not of one model. It appears for three constructions
with different architectures, and for each of them the gain over a grade-based reference is two to
three times the gain over a stage-based one. It replicated in an independent cohort in which grade
and stage both vary. What sets its size is not settled. Across the benchmark's five studies the gain
from swapping grade for stage tracks how uninformative grade is, which is a consistency check over
three points, but in the external cohorts the inflation was larger where grade varied, against the
protocol's prediction. The practical consequence does not depend on that and is a reporting rule. A multimodal survival model for this disease should report its added value over a
clinical reference that contains pathologic stage, and gains reported over grade alone should not be
compared with it.

The simplest combination wins here for a reason that can be stated in advance. The three arms are
close to independent, each is estimated with the events it can afford, and no event is spent on the
combination. Learned fusion spends events on weights, and a joint penalised model spends them on
1,049 coefficients at once. On this cohort both lose in every one of 24 re-partitions: narrowly to
stacking ($+0.0111$) and clearly to concatenation ($+0.0464$). Holding out whole hospitals widens both
gaps. This is the regime that sample-size guidance for prediction models anticipates. With 113 events
a method can afford few estimated quantities, and it should spend them on the modalities.

The ranking against published entries is a weaker statement, and we make it as one. ModRank has the
highest concordance reported on these folds, but it cannot be distinguished from the two competitors
that could be rerun with the same clinical variables, and a correctly specified correction for having
selected it among 206 candidates sets a bar it does not reach. Its utility over the clinical model is
suggestive, with intervals that mostly include zero. At this size the benchmark can separate a
correctly specified clinical reference from an incorrect one, and it cannot rank methods whose
published values differ by a few hundredths.

External evidence bounds the method claim. In three public cohorts without histology, the two-arm form
of the rule did not exceed the clinical stage block by more than each cohort's noise floor, and where
the microarray transcriptome carried no signal the equal-weight average fell below the stage block.
That is the cost of fitting nothing in the combination: an uninformative arm dilutes the average. A
gate that admits an arm only when it clears a permutation null inside the training fold removes the
dilution, but it cannot supply signal that the arms do not carry.

The study has further limitations. The only development cohort with slides is one public cohort
assembled across many hospitals, retrospectively, with an endpoint derived from curated follow-up.
ModRank ranks a validation fold within itself. A deployable variant that ranks each patient against
the training fold reaches 0.7247 against 0.7212, so this does not flatter the result. Part of its lead over the published entries
belongs to its slide encoder: given the tile features the incumbent consumed, it reaches 0.7009, which
is still above every published entry. Its calibration slope of 0.770 means that risks would be shrunk
by recalibration in a new setting. No analysis of performance across sociodemographic groups was done.
Deployment would need a digitised diagnostic slide, tumour expression profiling and the pathologic
stage of the resected specimen, which is available only after surgery.

What a reader can adopt now does not depend on the leaderboard. Clinical references for multimodal
survival models in bladder cancer should include pathologic stage, added value should be reported
against that reference, and at the sample sizes public cohorts provide, a fusion rule with nothing to
fit is a strong baseline that learned fusion has to beat on identical inputs before its gain is
attributed to the architecture.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Cohort, endpoint and prediction time point}

The development cohort is the bladder study of The Cancer Genome Atlas as partitioned by the
benchmark~\citep{jaume2024survpath}: 359 patients holding both a diagnostic whole-slide image and a
disease-specific survival label, 113 events and 24,219 comparable pairs. Time and event derive from
the Pan-Cancer Clinical Data Resource~\citep{liu2018tcgacdr}, which measures every endpoint from the
date of initial pathologic diagnosis. The tumours had not been exposed to chemotherapy when the
tissue was acquired~\citep{robertson2017blca}, and stage is the pathologic stage of the resected
specimen. The prediction is therefore post-operative. It can inform adjuvant treatment and follow-up.
It cannot inform the decision to give neoadjuvant chemotherapy, which precedes the specimen. The
study uses public, de-identified data only and required no ethical approval. Reporting follows
TRIPOD+AI (Supplementary Table~9).

\subsection*{Folds, representations and estimator}

The five fold files the benchmark released were used verbatim, and their membership is bound by
hash into the frozen protocol. We verified fold identity for each comparator where a repository
allowed it. Entries on other or unverifiable protocols are reported as context only
(Table~\ref{tab:published}).

\emph{Slide.} One 768-dimensional TITAN embedding per slide from a public precomputed
release~\citep{ding2025titan}, mean-pooled over a patient's slides. TITAN was pretrained without
patient outcomes and without TCGA slides. \emph{Transcriptome.} The benchmark's 4,999-gene matrix
averaged into the 275 of its 331 pathway groups that carry at least three of those genes.
\emph{Clinical.} Age, sex and AJCC pathologic tumour stage (one-hot) from the incumbent's released
split files, with histological grade from the same files for the grade construction. Missing levels
enter as the reference level, and no patient is dropped.

Each arm is a ridge-penalised Cox model~\citep{cox1972regression, simon2011regularization} fitted by
L-BFGS on the exact Breslow partial likelihood. The penalty is chosen from $\{1, 8, 64, 512, 4096\}$
by three-fold inner cross-validation inside the training fold, and standardisation uses
training-fold moments only. Out-of-fold linear predictors are mapped to within-fold percentiles, and
ModRank is the within-fold percentile of their equal-weight sum. A deployable variant that ranks a
new patient against the training fold's own scores reaches 0.7247 against the transductive 0.7212
(paired $p=0.69$).

\subsection*{Protocol, falsifiers and uncertainty}

The protocol fixed the arm, estimator, penalty grid, combination rule, comparator set, seeds and
decision rule after the development campaign and before the confirmatory run. Five leakage
falsifiers ran first: fold integrity, a mutation test of the standardisation boundary, a per-fold
planted label, row permutation and outcome provenance. Two were designed to reject and did. One
amendment rebuilt the clinical block from the incumbent's own files after a cached query was found
to have taken the first of several diagnosis records per patient. The amendment moved the primary
from 0.7260 to 0.7212. Concordance is Harrell's~\citep{harrell1982cindex}, pooled over out-of-fold
predictions after within-fold percentile normalisation. The primary is the mean over five
inner-cross-validation seeds, and paired comparisons use the seed-averaged score vector with 6,000
replicates that resample patients, never pairs. The noise floor is the standard deviation of a paired
difference over 24 random re-partitions (0.0073).

\subsection*{Fusion alternatives, re-partitions and held-out hospitals}

The concatenated model is one ridge Cox on all 1,049 columns with the penalty grid extended to
32,768, which is generous to it. The stacked model fits three weights (ridge 1.0) on the inner
out-of-fold percentiles of the three arms inside each training fold and applies them to the
validation percentiles. The re-partitions are the noise floor's own 24. For the hospital-grouped
analysis, whole tissue source sites (the second field of the patient identifier) were assigned to
five folds, largest first to the fold holding fewest patients.

\subsection*{Added value and its inflation}

For a construction $M$ and a clinical reference $R$, the added value is $C(M+R)-C(R)$, where $M+R$
combines them by the same rank rule. The inflation is $D = [C(M+\text{grade}) - C(\text{grade})] -
[C(M+\text{stage}) - C(\text{stage})]$. All four concordances are recomputed on each of 6,000
patient resamples. Competitors' scores are their own released out-of-fold predictions: SurvPath
averaged over five retrainings, and PIBD at its best-validation checkpoint, the convention behind its
published value.

\subsection*{Calibration and decision curves}

Inside each training fold, the training patients were scored by the same construction through a
three-fold inner split, one Cox coefficient on that score and a Breslow baseline hazard were fitted,
and both were applied to the validation patients to give survival at 12, 24 and 36 months. Reported:
calibration slope, calibration in the large (mean predicted against Kaplan--Meier observed risk at
each horizon), grouped calibration, the inverse-probability-of-censoring
weighted Brier score, the index of prediction accuracy against a training-fold Kaplan--Meier null,
and net benefit against treating all and none, estimated with Kaplan--Meier within the high-risk
group.

\subsection*{External cohorts}

Three public cohorts were selected by a joint-availability count of expression, stage, grade, age,
sex and a timed disease-specific outcome in the same samples, and three further series were excluded
(a duplicate, cell lines, a mixed neoadjuvant cohort). The analysis protocol, frozen before any of
their outcomes was analysed, specifies the endpoints, the clinical blocks, probe-to-gene mapping by
the platform annotation, the same 275 pathway groups, five repeats of stratified five-fold
cross-validation, and a noise floor from 24 re-partitions. An amendment recorded before two of the
three cohorts' results were inspected added an evidence-gated variant whose definition had been
fixed before any of these cohorts was downloaded.
