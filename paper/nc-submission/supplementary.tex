\noindent Half of the papers that could be read compare against no clinical variable at all, only
against other models. On this benchmark the comparison with the clinic is therefore either made
against a covariate that separates few of these patients, or not made.

\paragraph{Why grade separates few patients in this disease.} Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:entropy} sets the
two covariates side by side in each of the five studies the benchmark covers. Bladder is the extreme

takes two levels with 94.4\% of patients in one of them. A covariate that places almost every
patient in one category can separate few of them, whatever model is fitted on top of it.

\snote{The prespecified protocol, its amendment, the leakage checks and the selection correction}
\label{note:protocol}

\paragraph{The protocol.} The protocol fixed the arm, the estimator, the penalty grid, the
combination rule, the comparator set, the seeds and the decision rule. It was written after the
development campaign and the leakage falsifiers, and before the confirmatory run. Its rule was:
primary above $0.679 + 0.0239$, and a standard deviation across seeds of at most 0.0145, where
0.0239 is $\sigma\sqrt{2\ln N}$ with $\sigma = 0.0073$ and the $N = 211$ candidates the development
ledger held when the protocol was written. The second condition is a stability requirement and it
holds (0.0048 at amendment A1, 0.0043 after amendment A2). The first is mis-specified, as the last paragraph of this note shows.

\paragraph{Amendment A1, and its two opposite effects.} A cached clinical query had taken the first
of several diagnosis records per patient, and 61 of 359 patients changed once the correct record was
chosen. The record itself showed the problem before the check, since it carried stage IIA, IIB and IIC,
which are not American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) bladder stages. On seed 0, fixing the record
raises ModRank by $+0.0050$, and rebuilding the block from the incumbent's own released columns, which
drops the T, N and M categories the cached query had supplied, lowers it by 0.0110. Over the five
seeds the primary fell from 0.7260 to 0.7212, a move of $-0.0048$. The values in this paragraph are
as recorded at the time, and amendment A2 later moved the primary to 0.7214 (Supplementary
Note~\ref{note:amendA2}).

\paragraph{The leakage checks.} Five leakage checks ran before the confirmatory run, covering fold integrity,
a mutation test of the standardisation boundary, a per-fold planted label, row permutation and
outcome provenance. Two were built to reject and did. The per-fold planted label moves the score by
$-0.0006$.

\paragraph{The development campaign.} The campaign scored 269 candidate arms in a ledger generated
from the runs' own output. The protocol recorded 211 because that is what the ledger held when it was
written. Two further sub-experiments (58 rows) were scored afterwards and before the confirmatory
run. Not all 269 are candidates the frozen arm competed against: 26 are subgroup slices scored on part
of the cohort, 18 are in-sample oracles, 14 are permuted or null controls and 5 are cross-cohort
transfer probes. That leaves 206 full-cohort out-of-fold candidates. Among them the frozen
configuration is rank 11 counting every tie against it. Eight configurations score strictly above:
two are another TCGA study, three are components rejected on their own pre-registered controls, and
the rest are the declared transcriptomic sensitivity. The remaining two places are the frozen
construction's own duplicates, logged three times by three sub-experiments. Seven components were
designed, each with a falsifier and, where one could be built, a matched control. None cleared the
0.0145 bar (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:components} and Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:campaign}).

\paragraph{The selection correction was mis-specified.} A correction for taking the best of $N$
candidates, of the form $\sigma\sqrt{2\ln N}$, needs $\sigma$ to be the spread of one candidate's
own absolute score when no signal is present. The protocol substituted the spread of a paired difference between two arms
(0.0073), which is the quantity the noise floor measures and a different one. We measured the right
quantity directly. Across 200 permutations of the survival outcome, with folds, features and the
estimator untouched, the 14 fusion candidates of the final family score a mean of 0.5011 and a
standard deviation of 0.0398. The Gaussian bar with that $\sigma$ at $N = 206$ is 0.8089. The
empirical alternative needs no independence assumption, because the permuted candidates are
correlated through the same folds, features and estimator as the real search. The 95th percentile of the null maximum over the
family is 0.5918, an inflation of 0.0918 above chance, which sets the bar at 0.7708. The primary
(0.7214) clears neither, so no selection-corrected claim is made. On these folds ModRank has the
highest reported concordance by point estimate, and its paired comparisons with the two competitors
that could be rerun are not significant.

\paragraph{The biology the slide and transcriptome arms track.} Against eight standard axes of
muscle-invasive bladder cancer biology, the transcriptome arm tracks the molecular-class axes. Its
Spearman correlation is 0.3601 with the basal axis, $-0.372$ with the luminal axis and 0.3673 with the
epithelial--mesenchymal axis, all after Benjamini--Hochberg correction. The slide arm's correlation
with the basal axis is 0.0444 and does not survive correction. Its largest axis correlation is with
the FGFR3 axis ($-0.2236$), which carries no univariate prognostic signal in this cohort. Across the
275 pathway scores, 58 reach $q<0.05$ against the slide arm, and the two strongest are metabolic,
fatty acids ($-0.287$) and arachidonate production from diacylglycerol (0.278). These are
associations between an out-of-fold score and a pathway mean over 275 correlated tests. They say
what the score varies with, not that the model reads that pathway. No single pathway clears
Benjamini--Hochberg $q<0.10$ as a univariate predictor of disease-specific survival
(Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:landscape}), so the transcriptome arm's signal cannot be attributed
to any single pathway tested alone.

\paragraph{The two modalities are informative in different tumours.} The released files carry no
subtype call, so the cohort was split at the median of the basal-minus-luminal signature
difference. The axis is itself prognostic (log-rank $p=0.0012$). Inside the two halves the slide and
clinical arms exchange places, and ModRank moves far less than either
(Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:subtype} and Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:biology}).

\snote{The benchmark's other four studies}
\label{note:fivecohort}

\noindent The five studies the benchmark releases were scored with one recipe and one set of released
folds. The table gives every arm and every paired difference, each with the 95\% interval of 6,000
patient resamples. Beyond the three significant margins, the method is not separable from the
corrected clinical reference in stomach or colorectal, and the three-modality arm is not separable
from its two-modality form in any study.

\paragraph{Each study against its own re-partitioning noise.} A patient-resampling interval
describes how a margin varies when the patients are resampled. It does not describe how the margin
varies across partitions of the same patients, which is what the bladder floor in the main text
measures. Every study was
therefore re-partitioned 24 times by the construction that built that floor, refitting all three
arms inside each partition. The paired difference is positive in 24 of 24 partitions in
every study. The standard deviation of that difference, and twice it as the bar, are 0.0116 and
0.0232 in bladder, 0.0238 and 0.0475 in breast, 0.0133 and 0.0267 in colorectal, 0.0135 and 0.0269
in head and neck, and 0.0162 and 0.0325 in stomach. Four margins exceed their own bar. Colorectal
does not, at $+0.0238$ against 0.0267. Only in stomach do the two criteria disagree. Its margin of
$+0.0449$ clears the bar of 0.0325, while its bootstrap interval covers zero.

The bladder floor here is 0.0116, under the benchmark's released clinical file and one seed, against
0.0073 in the main text, under the incumbent's clinical block and five seeds. Having bladder measured both ways is what makes the
other four floors comparable to a number the paper already carries.

\paragraph{The same five studies under a second slide encoder.} The slide arm is one pretrained
encoder, and the paper already reports that this encoder is worth about 0.02 on the development
cohort, so the validation invites the question of whether three studies separate because of the
recipe or because of that encoder. All five were therefore scored again with a self-supervised
encoder trained by another group on a different objective, chosen because it is the one alternative
on hand that covers every study, with features for 359 bladder, 863 breast, 298 colorectal, 394 head
and neck and 318 stomach cases. The folds, clinical block, transcriptome arm, estimator, penalty grid
and bootstrap are unchanged, and every comparison within this run uses the same patients. Breast and
stomach lose 8 and 1 patients for whom the alternative encoder has no features, so their values under
the two encoders rest on slightly different patient sets.

Concordance falls in the three studies that separate, bladder from 0.7003 to 0.6794, breast from 0.6881 to 0.6529 and head and neck
from 0.6179 to 0.6029, and it rises slightly in the two that do not, stomach from 0.6290 to 0.6357
and colorectal from 0.7140 to 0.7208.

The margins over the clinical arm fall with them and the same three studies still separate: bladder
$+0.0574$ $[+0.0044, +0.1101]$, breast $+0.0849$ $[+0.0163, +0.1531]$, head and neck $+0.0576$
$[+0.0070, +0.1094]$. Stomach and colorectal cover zero under both encoders, and the grade-control
comparison agrees under both as well, separating in colorectal alone.

This does not establish that either encoder is adequate, or that a third would agree. It is one
alternative, chosen for coverage rather than for being the best available, and it settles only that
the three-of-five conclusion does not depend on which of these two encoders is used.

\paragraph{The fusion alternatives, on all five studies.} The development cohort compares the rank
average against a concatenated Cox and a stacked Cox and is ahead of both in 24 of 24 re-partitions,
by more than the bladder bar against the concatenation and by less against the stacking. The
other four studies never had that comparison, so a statement about what fitting the combination buys
rested on one study in five. Both alternatives were therefore built on all five. The concatenation
was given a penalty grid extended to 32,768, and the stacking learned three weights per fold from the
arms' inner out-of-fold percentiles, so the weights never saw the scores they weight. Three further
fitted fusions were specified after these results were known and before they were run. Each is fitted
only inside the training fold on the same inner out-of-fold percentiles. The first chooses
non-negative weights summing to one from a grid of step 0.1 by inner concordance, the second adds the
three pairwise products of the arm percentiles to the stacked model, and the third chooses separate
grid weights within each tertile of the clinical arm's inner out-of-fold percentile. Before any of them was scored, the rank average reproduced its
committed value in every study, and the stacking rebuilt from the same inner percentiles reproduced
its committed value.

\snote{The external cohorts}
\label{note:geo}

\paragraph{Selection.} Public bladder series in the Gene Expression Omnibus were admitted when
expression, stage, age, sex and a timed disease-specific outcome were recorded for the same samples.
Grade, which the inflation analysis needs, was counted as well. The count was made from the series'
characteristic fields before any outcome was analysed. Three cohorts were admitted: GSE31684 (93 patients treated by radical cystectomy, Affymetrix
GPL570)~\citep{riester2012combination}, GSE32894 (the Lund cohort of non-muscle-invasive and
muscle-invasive tumours, Illumina GPL6947)~\citep{sjodahl2012molecular} and GSE48075 (GPL6947, no
grade field, so used for the combination rule only)~\citep{choi2014identification}. Three further
series were excluded: one repeats the samples of GSE48075, one holds cell lines, and one mixes
patients given neoadjuvant chemotherapy into the cohort and records no grade.

\paragraph{Protocol.} The protocol was fixed before any outcome of these cohorts was analysed. It
fixes the endpoints (death from bladder cancer), the clinical blocks, probe-to-gene mapping from the
platform annotation, the same pathway definitions as the benchmark (keeping groups with at least
three genes present), five repeats of stratified five-fold cross-validation, paired comparisons on
repeat-averaged scores with 6,000 patient resamples and Holm correction within each cohort, and a
noise floor from 24 re-partitions. The recipe is refitted inside each cohort. No coefficient fitted
on the development cohort is transferred across platforms. Before the run, the protocol predicted
that the inflation $D$ would be smaller in the Lund cohort, where grade varies, than in the
cystectomy cohort. It was larger.

every arm. Where the microarray transcriptome arm is informative (Lund, GSE48075), two-arm ModRank has
the best point estimate but sits inside the bar of the clinical stage block. In the Lund cohort it
exceeded the transcriptome arm and the concatenated model after Holm correction ($p=0.003$ and $p=0.006$).
Where the arm is uninformative (the cystectomy cohort, 0.4747), the equal-weight average is pulled
below the stage block. An equal-weight rule is exposed to this failure whenever one arm carries
nothing.

\paragraph{The permutation-screened variant.} The variant admits an arm into the average only if, inside
the training fold, its inner out-of-fold concordance exceeds the 95th percentile of 1,000
permutations of the training outcomes. Its definition was fixed before any of these cohorts was
downloaded. It was added to this analysis by a dated amendment after the cystectomy cohort's results
had been read and before the other two cohorts' results were opened, so it was tested only on those
two. In the Lund cohort both arms met the permutation criterion in every fold and the variant
equalled ModRank (0.8792). In GSE48075 the screen dropped the transcriptome arm in 16 of 25 folds and the stage block in 8,
and the variant fell below both (0.6170 against 0.6334 for the block and 0.6389 for ModRank). It did not clear the bar
in either cohort. On the development cohort all three arms meet the criterion and the variant is
identical to ModRank. The screen is meant to prevent dilution by an uninformative arm. It removes arms
and supplies no information of its own. An average of weakly correlated arms can exceed its own best arm, and
in the Lund cohort it does, 0.8792 against 0.8672 for the stage block. In none of these cohorts did
any combination exceed the clinical stage block by more than the floor.

\snote{The inflation against a score without information}
\label{note:nulld}

\paragraph{The need for a benchmark.} Under the rank rule a model $M$ and a reference $R$ are
combined as the within-fold percentile of $M+R$. An equal-weight average dilutes a strong reference
more than a weak one, so a score that carries no information gains less over the stage-based
reference than over the grade-based one, and its $D$ is positive. $D$ tested against zero therefore
mixes a property of the combination rule with what the model adds.

The benchmark keeps each reference
and the rule and replaces the model by random within-fold ranks, one random arm for a two-arm
construction and two for ModRank's three-arm sum. Its $D$ is the mean over 2,000 random draws. The
excess, $D$ minus that mean, has an interval from 6,000 case resamples drawn as for every $D$, on each
of which the benchmark is averaged over 20 fixed random draws. In the external cohorts the random arm
replaces the transcriptome within each repeat of cross-validation. Every construction's $D$ was
reproduced from its committed per-case scores before its benchmark was computed. The benchmark was
specified after the analyses it qualifies.

\paragraph{Four further architectures.} To see whether $D$ depends on the architecture, four
multimodal baselines of the SurvPath code base were retrained on the released folds from the same
768-dimensional tile features. They are MCAT, with co-attention between pathways and tiles, and
ABMIL, TransMIL and DeepMISL, each with pathway inputs and concatenation fusion. Learning rate and number of epochs
are those of each model's released script, and each model was retrained five times under a protocol
committed before the runs. The released models expect 1,024-dimensional tile features, so their
constructors were given the feature dimension, the same repair the SurvPath rerun needed. MOTCat is
not implemented in the released code and was not run. Each model's five retrainings were ranked
within fold and averaged, and the average was combined with each reference by the rank rule. These
are implementations of the four architectures under this pipeline, not reproductions of their
original authors' tuned models or results.

\snote{The reference gap in external bladder cohorts}

survival)~\citep{kim2010progression, lee2010e2f1} and E-MTAB-4321 (non-muscle-invasive, progression to
muscle invasion)~\citep{hedegaard2016uromol}. Two series were not admitted because their deposited

\snote{Amendment A2: the Cox risk sets and tied ranks}
\label{note:amendA2}

\paragraph{The defects.} Review found two defects in the survival functions that every arm passes
through. The Cox fit indexed each event's risk set by the event's own row in the time-sorted data, so
patients who shared the event's time but were sorted before it were left out. The objective was
therefore not the Breslow partial likelihood that the protocol states, and it depended on the order
of tied rows. The development cohort has 36 tied times, 24 of them with an event, covering 75
patients. Within-fold percentiles broke exact ties in the order of NumPy's default sort, which is
unspecified and differs between builds. The corrected fit gives every event all patients whose time
is at least its own, and tied scores share their average rank. Before use, the corrected objective
was checked against an exhaustive computation of the Breslow likelihood and shown to be invariant to
the order of the rows. It equals the original objective when no times tie, and its gradient agrees
with finite differences.

\paragraph{The procedure.} The amendment's protocol was written before any result was regenerated.
Each script that produces a reported result was first run with the original functions and had to
reproduce its committed output, and was then run with the corrected ones. Folds, seeds, penalty
grids, bootstrap draws and every threshold were unchanged. The development campaign, the
confirmatory run (0.7260) and the constants of the frozen protocol, including the noise floor of
0.0073, are reported as recorded. One comparator was added, stacking whose ridge is chosen from
$\{0.1, 1, 10, 100\}$ by three-fold cross-validation over the inner out-of-fold percentiles. On the
development cohort it reaches $0.7147 \pm 0.0048$, and ModRank exceeds it by $+0.0055$
($[-0.0071, +0.0180]$). In none of the five studies does its difference from the rank average have
an interval that excludes zero (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:fusion}).

\paragraph{What moved.} Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:amendA2} lists the headline quantities before
and after. Most moved by less than 0.003. The larger movements follow from ties. DeepMISL's risks were
constant within the validation fold in 13 of its 25 retraining--fold pairs. Ranked by row order,
those ties had passed the order of the patients into its concordance, whereas with shared ranks a
constant fold adds nothing, so its $D$ fell from $+0.034$ to $+0.005$ (Supplementary
Note~\ref{note:nulld}). The stage and grade models of GSE19915 predict from a few categories, and
their gap rose from $+0.129$ to $+0.161$.

\paragraph{What changed in the text.} Four statements changed with these values. Five of the six external
cohorts, not four, now give the stage-based reference an interval that excludes zero. Three of the
four further architectures, not four, have a $D$ resolved from zero, and DeepMISL's excess is below
zero. In GSE48075 the permutation-screened variant fell below the stage block instead of approaching
it. The selection bar moved from 0.7716 to 0.7708, and the primary still does not clear it. No other
count, ordering or significance statement in the manuscript changed.

\snote{The search for an independent cohort with slides, expression and survival}\label{note:search}

The slide arm could be tested outside the development cohort only in a cohort that records a
diagnostic whole-slide image, bulk tumour expression and a timed outcome for the same patients,
ideally with stage, grade, age and sex, and at least 30 events. We searched for one in September 2026
under a plan written before the search began, which fixed these requirements, the resources to cover
and a budget of about 150 queries. A tissue microarray was to be recorded separately from whole
slides, and a cohort with slides for some patients and expression for others did not qualify. No
data were downloaded or requested during the search. Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:search} lists what
was covered and found.

The result describes this search, not every archive. Several portals returned no usable interface
and were searched only through the public web index, and the records of the 22 expression series
held in the Gene Expression Omnibus could not be opened in the search session. The earlier
enumeration had already recorded them as expression cohorts without slides. Of the 33 expression
cohorts, 31 were screened for slides and two were excluded in advance, the development cohort and
one controlled study.

One trial cohort holds all three. SWOG S1314 randomised 237 patients with cT2--T4aN0M0 urothelial
carcinoma to neoadjuvant dose-dense MVAC or gemcitabine--cisplatin before cystectomy, 227 of them
eligible, with a median follow-up of 53 months~\citep{flaig2021s1314}. A later analysis used 182
pretreatment H\&E whole-slide images and microarray expression from 180 of these
patients~\citep{bai2025gmlf}. Its expression data are public, but the slides are held by the trial
group and released to qualified researchers on request, and outcome data follow the group's data
request procedure, which requires committee approval and a data use agreement. It would test a
different setting from the development cohort: every patient received chemotherapy, the stage
available before treatment is clinical rather than pathologic, and the number of deaths among the
patients with both slides and expression is not published.

One bladder challenge dataset also holds all three. Its rules bar publishing results obtained with it
before the organisers' papers appear, and we report nothing from it.
